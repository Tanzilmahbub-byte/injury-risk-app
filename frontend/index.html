    Why ACWR & workload matter
    from the literature review

    The Acute:Chronic Workload Ratio (ACWR) compares a player's recent training/match load against their longer-term average — a sudden spike well above what the body is conditioned for is a well-established early warning sign in injury-forecasting research. This dashboard's Training_Hours_Per_Week and Matches_Played_Past_Season fields are the closest workload proxies available in data.csv, and both show up in the Random Forest's feature importance ranking above.

    Limitations
    read before trusting these numbers
    No shared key between datasets: data.csv (anonymous player attributes) and player_injuries_impact.csv (named injury case history) don't share a player ID, so risk predictions here are trained only on data.csv — the Injury Insights page uses the injuries file separately and honestly, not merged in.

    High accuracy: this dataset's target looks cleanly separable, which is why all three models score well — treat these numbers as a demonstration of the pipeline, not a validated real-world risk model.

    Small dataset: 800 rows is workable for Logistic Regression / Random Forest but modest for a neural net.
